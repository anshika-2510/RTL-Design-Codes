# Stage 1: Crystal structure, tensors, and why symmetry sets the optics of SBN

Main references: J.-M. Liu, *Nonlinear Photonics* (CUP 2022), Ch. 1 to 3 and Ch. 11. P. Yeh, *Photorefractive Nonlinear Optics*. J. F. Nye, *Physical Properties of Crystals*.

Numbers for SBN (lattice constants, r coefficients, Tc) are ballpark. They shift with Sr fraction, poling, and clamped vs unclamped conditions. Check them against a table for your exact composition.

---

## 0. Conventions

- Axes $`x, y, z`$ with $`z \parallel c`$ (the polar axis).
- Liu writes $`\boldsymbol\epsilon = \epsilon_0(\mathbf I + \boldsymbol\chi)`$ (Eq. 1.33 and 1.49), so $`\varepsilon_r = 1 + \chi`$.
- Voigt (contracted) index, used for $`d_{il}`$, $`r_{Ik}`$ and the impermeability:

```math
xx \to 1,\quad yy \to 2,\quad zz \to 3,\quad yz \to 4,\quad zx \to 5,\quad xy \to 6
```

- Liu's complex field is $`\mathcal E = E + \text{c.c.}`$ with **no** factor of 1/2 (Eq. 1.37). So his $`\chi^{(2)}`$ and $`d`$ differ by factors of 2 from books that use $`\tfrac12(E+\text{c.c.})`$. Keep this in mind when comparing numbers.

## 1. The crystal itself

### 1.1 Structure

Formula: $`\mathrm{Sr}_x\mathrm{Ba}_{1-x}\mathrm{Nb_2O_6}`$ with $`x \approx 0.25`$ to $`0.75`$. "SBN:61" means $`x = 0.61`$.

It is a **tetragonal tungsten bronze (TTB)**:

- The skeleton is corner-sharing $`\mathrm{NbO_6}`$ octahedra. They form straight Nb-O-Nb chains along $`c`$ and are linked in the plane.
- The framework leaves three kinds of channels along $`c`$: square (A1), pentagonal (A2) and small triangular (C).
- Sr and Ba sit in A1 and A2. A cell has 6 A sites but only 5 cations, so on average one site per cell is empty. The general cell is $`(\mathrm{A1})_2(\mathrm{A2})_4\mathrm{C}_4\mathrm{Nb}_{10}\mathrm{O}_{30}`$. That built-in disorder is why SBN is a **relaxor**: the phase transition is smeared and the permittivity peak is broad.
- Cell size (rough): $`a \approx 12.4`$ to $`12.5`$ Å and $`c \approx 3.9`$ Å. The cell is wide and short. This matters in section 6.

### 1.2 Phase transition and point group

| Phase | Point group | Space group | Inversion? |
|---|---|---|---|
| above Tc (paraelectric) | 4/mmm | P4/mbm | yes |
| below Tc (ferroelectric) | 4mm | P4bm | **no** |

Tc is roughly 50 to 150 °C and goes down as Sr content goes up.

Below Tc the Nb atoms shift off-centre along $`+c`$ or $`-c`$ (the domain direction). That kills the inversion centre and the mirror plane perpendicular to $`c`$, and leaves a spontaneous polarization $`P_s \parallel c`$. This is why SBN is polar and has a linear electro-optic (Pockels) effect. A centrosymmetric crystal can't (section 4.0).

### 1.3 The eight operations of 4mm

4mm has a 4-fold axis along $`z`$ plus mirror planes containing $`z`$:

```math
\{E,\; C_2,\; C_4,\; C_4^{3},\; m_x,\; m_y,\; m_d,\; m_{d'}\}
```

As matrices acting on $`(x,y,z)`$:

```math
C_4 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
C_2 = C_4^2 = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
```

```math
m_x = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
m_y = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
```

$`m_d`$ and $`m_{d'}`$ swap $`x \leftrightarrow \pm y`$. Everything is generated by $`C_4`$ and $`m_x`$, so those two are enough for the derivations below.

## 2. Tensors and Neumann's principle

### 2.1 Transformation law

Under a rotation or reflection $`R`$ with components $`R_{ia}`$, a rank-$`n`$ tensor changes as

```math
T'_{ijk\ldots} = R_{ia} R_{jb} R_{kc} \cdots\, T_{abc\ldots}
```

For rank 2 this is $`\mathbf T' = R\,\mathbf T\,R^{\mathsf T}`$.

### 2.2 Neumann's principle

> Any macroscopic property tensor of a crystal must be unchanged by every symmetry operation of the crystal's point group.

So for every $`R`$ in 4mm we need $`T' = T`$. That gives linear equations on the components, and most components get forced to zero or tied together. This is the whole mechanism behind "symmetry tells you which components survive."

### 2.3 Shortcut: parity counting

$`C_2`$, $`m_x`$ and $`m_y`$ are diagonal ($`\pm1`$), so each component just picks up a sign:

- Under $`C_2`$: the sign is $`(-1)^{n_\perp}`$, where $`n_\perp`$ is how many indices are $`x`$ or $`y`$. The component survives only if $`n_\perp`$ is **even**.
- Under $`m_x`$: the sign is $`(-1)^{n_x}`$. It survives only if the number of $`x`$ indices is even.
- Under $`m_y`$: same with $`y`$.
- $`C_4`$ ($`x \to y`$, $`y \to -x`$) then ties the leftovers together.

This gets every result below in a few lines.

## 3. Rank 1 and rank 2 tensors

### 3.1 Vector: spontaneous polarization

$`p_x`$ and $`p_y`$ have $`n_\perp = 1`$, so $`C_2`$ kills them. Only $`p_z`$ survives:

```math
\mathbf P_s = (0,\,0,\,P_s)
```

A polar vector is allowed only in the 10 polar point groups, and 4mm is one of them.

### 3.2 Symmetric rank 2: permittivity, permeability, inverse effective mass

Do it explicitly once with $`\boldsymbol\varepsilon' = R\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon\,R^{\mathsf T}`$ and $`R = C_4`$.

Take $`\varepsilon_{xz}`$. Row $`x`$ of $`C_4`$ is $`(0,-1,0)`$ and row $`z`$ is $`(0,0,1)`$, so

```math
\varepsilon'_{xz} = -\varepsilon_{yz}, \qquad \varepsilon'_{yz} = +\varepsilon_{xz}
```

Invariance needs $`\varepsilon_{xz} = -\varepsilon_{yz}`$ and $`\varepsilon_{yz} = \varepsilon_{xz}`$. Together: $`\varepsilon_{xz} = -\varepsilon_{xz}`$, so $`\varepsilon_{xz} = \varepsilon_{yz} = 0`$.

The same rotation gives $`\varepsilon_{xx} = \varepsilon_{yy}`$ and $`\varepsilon_{xy} = -\varepsilon_{yx}`$. The tensor is symmetric, so $`\varepsilon_{xy} = 0`$.

```math
\boldsymbol\varepsilon_r =
\begin{pmatrix}
\varepsilon_\perp & 0 & 0 \\
0 & \varepsilon_\perp & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \varepsilon_\parallel
\end{pmatrix}
```

Two independent numbers. That is a **uniaxial** crystal with the optic axis along $`c`$. At optical frequencies:

```math
n_o = \sqrt{\varepsilon_\perp}, \qquad n_e = \sqrt{\varepsilon_\parallel}
```

For SBN:61 at 633 nm, roughly $`n_o \approx 2.31`$ and $`n_e \approx 2.30`$. So it is negative uniaxial ($`n_e < n_o`$) with a very small birefringence, $`|\Delta n| \sim 0.01`$. This small number matters in section 5.4.

**Permeability.** At optical frequency $`\mathbf M \approx 0`$, so $`\boldsymbol\mu = \mu_0\mathbf I`$ (Liu Eq. 1.30). At low frequency $`\boldsymbol\mu`$ would also have the form $`\mathrm{diag}(\mu_\perp,\mu_\perp,\mu_\parallel)`$, but SBN is nonmagnetic, so only $`\varepsilon`$ matters in practice.

**Same argument, other properties.** Any symmetric rank-2 tensor has this diagonal form in 4mm: thermal expansion, conductivity, and (for section 6) the inverse effective-mass tensor.

## 4. Rank 3 tensors: chi2, d and the Pockels tensor

### 4.0 Inversion kills every rank-3 tensor

Inversion sends $`\mathbf r \to -\mathbf r`$, so every index flips sign: $`T'_{ijk} = (-1)^3 T_{ijk} = -T_{ijk}`$. If the crystal has inversion, $`T = -T`$, so $`T = 0`$.

In the **paraelectric** (4/mmm) phase: $`\chi^{(2)} = 0`$ and there is no Pockels effect. Only the quadratic (Kerr-type) EO effect exists. Dropping to 4mm is what allows the linear effect.

### 4.1 4mm rank 3 by parity counting

Take a general $`T_{ijk}`$ with no index symmetry yet.

1. **$`C_2`$**: need $`n_\perp`$ even. Survivors: $`zzz`$, and anything with exactly two transverse indices and one $`z`$ (like $`xxz`$, $`xzx`$, $`zxx`$, $`yyz`$, $`xyz`$).
2. **$`m_x`$, $`m_y`$**: kill anything with an odd number of $`x`$'s or an odd number of $`y`$'s. That removes the $`xyz`$-type components. Left: $`zzz`$, $`xxz`$, $`xzx`$, $`zxx`$, $`yyz`$, $`yzy`$, $`zyy`$.
3. **$`C_4`$**: $`xxz = yyz`$, $`xzx = yzy`$, $`zxx = zyy`$.

So a general rank-3 tensor has four independent components: $`T_{zzz}`$, $`T_{xxz}`$, $`T_{xzx}`$, $`T_{zxx}`$.

### 4.2 The d tensor (SHG)

With intrinsic permutation symmetry (Liu §3.4.1), $`\chi^{(2)}_{ijk}`$ is symmetric under $`j \leftrightarrow k`$, so $`\chi_{xxz} = \chi_{xzx}`$ and three components remain. In contracted notation:

```math
d =
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & d_{15} & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & d_{15} & 0 & 0 \\
d_{31} & d_{31} & d_{33} & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{pmatrix}
```

(The $`C_4`$ step gives $`d_{24} = d_{15}`$ and $`d_{32} = d_{31}`$.) With Kleinman symmetry (far from resonance, Liu §3.4.3), all three indices permute freely and $`d_{15} = d_{31}`$, leaving two independent numbers: $`d_{31}`$ and $`d_{33}`$.

### 4.3 The Pockels tensor

Different index symmetry: the first two indices $`(ij)`$ are symmetric (the impermeability is symmetric), and the third is the field index. Parity counting on $`r_{ij,k}`$:

- After $`C_2`$, $`m_x`$, $`m_y`$ the survivors are $`(xx,z)`$, $`(yy,z)`$, $`(zz,z)`$, $`(xz,x)`$, $`(yz,y)`$.
- $`C_4`$ gives $`(xx,z) = (yy,z)`$ and $`(xz,x) = (yz,y)`$.

As a 6 by 3 matrix $`r_{Ik}`$:

```math
r =
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 0 & r_{13} \\
0 & 0 & r_{13} \\
0 & 0 & r_{33} \\
0 & r_{51} & 0 \\
r_{51} & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0
\end{pmatrix}
```

Three independent coefficients: $`r_{13}`$, $`r_{33}`$, $`r_{51}`$. This has the same structure as the piezoelectric tensor in 4mm, which is why clamped and unclamped $`r`$ differ in SBN.

### 4.4 Link between r and chi2

The linear EO effect is the $`\chi^{(2)}(\omega;\,\omega,0)`$ process. In the usual convention

```math
r_{ijk} = -\frac{2}{n_i^{2}\, n_j^{2}}\,\chi^{(2)}_{ijk}(\omega;\omega,0)
```

The factor of 2 depends on the complex-field convention (section 0). Check Liu §11.1.4 for his exact form before using numbers.

## 5. Index ellipsoid and delta n

### 5.1 Where the ellipsoid comes from

Electric energy density: $`w = \tfrac12 \mathbf E\cdot\mathbf D`$. With $`\mathbf D = \epsilon_0\boldsymbol\varepsilon_r \mathbf E`$,

```math
w = \frac{1}{2\epsilon_0}\; \mathbf D \cdot \boldsymbol\varepsilon_r^{-1} \cdot \mathbf D
```

Define the impermeability $`B = \boldsymbol\varepsilon_r^{-1}`$ and normalise $`\mathbf x = \mathbf D/\sqrt{2\epsilon_0 w}`$. The surface of constant energy is

```math
\sum_{ij} B_{ij}\, x_i x_j = 1
```

For $`\mathbf D`$ along a unit vector $`\hat s`$, $`B_{ij}\hat s_i \hat s_j = 1/n^2`$, so the semi-axis in direction $`\hat s`$ is the refractive index for that polarization. For a wave travelling along $`\mathbf k`$, cut the ellipsoid with the plane through the origin perpendicular to $`\mathbf k`$. The axes of that ellipse are the two normal-mode indices (Liu §4.3.1).

At zero field in SBN:

```math
\frac{x^2 + y^2}{n_o^2} + \frac{z^2}{n_e^2} = 1
```

### 5.2 Adding a field

To first order,

```math
\Delta B_{I} = \sum_k r_{Ik}\,E_k
```

With the 4mm matrix from 4.3:

```math
\begin{aligned}
\Delta B_1 = \Delta B_2 &= r_{13}E_z \\
\Delta B_3 &= r_{33}E_z \\
\Delta B_4 &= r_{51}E_y \\
\Delta B_5 &= r_{51}E_x \\
\Delta B_6 &= 0
\end{aligned}
```

The ellipsoid becomes

```math
\left(\frac{1}{n_o^2} + r_{13}E_z\right)(x^2+y^2)
+ \left(\frac{1}{n_e^2} + r_{33}E_z\right) z^2
+ 2r_{51}E_y\, yz + 2r_{51}E_x\, zx = 1
```

The factors of 2 on the cross terms appear because $`B_{yz}`$ and $`B_{zy}`$ both contribute.

### 5.3 Field along c

With $`E_x = E_y = 0`$ there are no cross terms, so the axes don't rotate and SBN stays uniaxial. Use $`d(1/n^2) = -2\,dn/n^3`$:

```math
\delta n_o = -\tfrac12\, n_o^3\, r_{13}\, E_z,
\qquad
\delta n_e = -\tfrac12\, n_e^3\, r_{33}\, E_z
```

This is the $`\delta n = \tfrac12 n_e^3 r_{33} E`$ form from your notes. The minus sign comes from $`\Delta(1/n^2) = rE`$ (a positive $`rE`$ lowers $`n`$). Papers often drop it and quote the magnitude.

Rough feel: $`r_{33} = 200`$ pm/V, $`n_e = 2.3`$, $`E = 10^6`$ V/m (1 kV/mm):

```math
|\delta n_e| = \tfrac12 (2.3)^3 (200\times10^{-12})(10^{6}) \approx 1.2\times10^{-3}
```

Since $`r_{33} \gg r_{13}`$ (roughly an order of magnitude), light polarised along $`c`$ sees most of the effect. That's why the usual geometry is $`E \parallel c`$ with extraordinary polarization.

In a photorefractive crystal the field is the internal space-charge field $`E_{sc}(\mathbf r)`$, so the index grating is $`\delta n(\mathbf r) \propto -\tfrac12 n_e^3 r_{\mathrm{eff}} E_{sc}(\mathbf r)`$. That is the link to Yeh's book.

### 5.4 Field along x (or y): the axes rotate

With only $`E_x`$, the x-z block of the ellipsoid matrix is

```math
\begin{pmatrix} 1/n_o^2 & r_{51}E_x \\ r_{51}E_x & 1/n_e^2 \end{pmatrix}
```

For a symmetric 2 by 2 matrix with entries $`a, b`$ on the diagonal and $`c`$ off it, the principal axes are rotated by $`\theta`$ with $`\tan 2\theta = 2c/(a-b)`$. So

```math
\tan 2\theta = \frac{2\,r_{51}E_x}{1/n_o^2 - 1/n_e^2}
```

SBN's birefringence is tiny, so the denominator is tiny (about $`-1.6\times10^{-3}`$ for $`n_o=2.31`$, $`n_e=2.30`$). With $`r_{51} \approx 40`$ pm/V and $`E = 10^6`$ V/m the numerator is about $`8\times10^{-5}`$, so $`|\tan2\theta| \approx 0.05`$ and $`\theta \approx 1.4^\circ`$. Not huge, but far more than in a strongly birefringent crystal. The nearly degenerate $`n_o \approx n_e`$ makes the axes easy to tilt.

## 6. How crystal geometry controls band structure, and then the optical tensors

Plan: (a) what the lattice does to $`E(\mathbf k)`$, (b) how $`E(\mathbf k)`$ gives $`\varepsilon_{ij}(\omega)`$, (c) how the ferroelectric distortion shows up in both.

### 6.1 Bloch states and the Brillouin zone

Translation symmetry gives Bloch's theorem:

```math
\psi_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r) = e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r}\,u_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r),
\qquad
u_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r + \mathbf R) = u_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)
```

$`E_n(\mathbf k) = E_n(\mathbf k + \mathbf G)`$ for any reciprocal lattice vector $`\mathbf G`$, so $`\mathbf k`$ only needs to cover the first Brillouin zone (BZ). For a tetragonal cell $`a\times a\times c`$:

```math
\mathbf b_1 = \frac{2\pi}{a}\hat x,\quad
\mathbf b_2 = \frac{2\pi}{a}\hat y,\quad
\mathbf b_3 = \frac{2\pi}{c}\hat z
```

High-symmetry points: $`\Gamma = (0,0,0)`$, $`X = (\pi/a, 0, 0)`$, $`M = (\pi/a, \pi/a, 0)`$, $`Z = (0, 0, \pi/c)`$, $`R = (\pi/a, 0, \pi/c)`$, $`A = (\pi/a, \pi/a, \pi/c)`$.

**Geometry consequence with SBN numbers.** With $`a \approx 12.4`$ Å and $`c \approx 3.9`$ Å:

```math
\frac{\pi}{a} \approx 0.25\ \text{\AA}^{-1},
\qquad
\frac{\pi}{c} \approx 0.8\ \text{\AA}^{-1}
```

A big real-space cell means a small BZ. The in-plane zone is tiny, so bands are heavily **folded** (many bands packed into a small zone), while along $`z`$ the zone is about 3 times longer. DFT band plots of TTB niobates look dense for this reason: the cell has 10 Nb and 30 O.

### 6.2 What the point group does in k-space

The point group acts on $`\mathbf k`$ too:

```math
E_n(R\mathbf k) = E_n(\mathbf k) \quad \text{for } R \in 4mm
```

Time reversal gives $`E_n(\mathbf k) = E_n(-\mathbf k)`$ (ignoring spin-orbit), so even without inversion the bands are symmetric about $`\Gamma`$.

At $`\Gamma`$ the little group is the full $`C_{4v}`$. States are labelled by its irreps $`A_1, A_2, B_1, B_2, E`$, where $`E`$ is 2-fold degenerate. Along $`\Gamma\to Z`$ (the $`c`$ axis) the full $`C_{4v}`$ is kept, so $`E`$ doublets stay degenerate. Along $`\Gamma\to X`$ only the identity and one mirror plane survive, so the $`E`$ doublet **splits**. Splitting of degenerate bands off the $`c`$ axis is a direct consequence of the 4-fold axis.

With spin-orbit coupling, a missing inversion centre plus a polar axis also allows Rashba-type spin splitting. Skip this for now.

### 6.3 Anisotropic effective mass (tight-binding toy)

Take a simple lattice with Nb-Nb spacing $`d`$, in-plane hopping $`t_\perp`$ and hopping along $`c`$ of $`t_\parallel`$:

```math
E(\mathbf k) = E_0 - 2t_\perp\left[\cos(k_x d) + \cos(k_y d)\right] - 2t_\parallel\cos(k_z d)
```

Near the band minimum:

```math
\left(\frac{1}{m^{\ast}}\right)_{ij} = \frac{1}{\hbar^2}\frac{\partial^2 E}{\partial k_i\partial k_j}
\;\Rightarrow\;
m_\perp = \frac{\hbar^2}{2t_\perp d^2},\quad
m_\parallel = \frac{\hbar^2}{2t_\parallel d^2}
```

So $`\mathbf m^{\ast} = \mathrm{diag}(m_\perp, m_\perp, m_\parallel)`$, the same form as $`\boldsymbol\varepsilon`$ in section 3 (same Neumann argument for a symmetric rank-2 tensor). Nothing in 4mm forces $`t_\perp = t_\parallel`$: bond lengths and Nb-O-Nb geometry differ in-plane vs along $`c`$, so bands disperse differently. This is a cartoon. The real TTB framework has a big cell and several Nb sites with lower local symmetry, so don't read quantitative numbers from it.

### 6.4 The ferroelectric distortion and the gap (two-band toy)

Take a 1D chain along $`c`$ with alternating O and Nb sites. Hopping between Nb and the neighbouring O depends on bond length. If every Nb shifts up by $`\delta`$, the bonds along the chain go **short, long, short, long**, with hoppings $`t_s > t_l`$. Two sites per cell (O 2p and Nb 4d), with on-site energies $`\varepsilon_p`$, $`\varepsilon_d`$ and $`\Delta = \varepsilon_d - \varepsilon_p`$:

```math
H(k) =
\begin{pmatrix}
\varepsilon_d & V(k) \\
V^{\ast}(k) & \varepsilon_p
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
V(k) = t_s + t_l\, e^{ikc}
```

```math
E_\pm(k) = \frac{\varepsilon_d + \varepsilon_p}{2} \pm \sqrt{\frac{\Delta^2}{4} + \lvert V(k)\rvert^2},
\qquad
\lvert V\rvert^2 = t_s^2 + t_l^2 + 2t_st_l\cos kc
```

The minimum gap is at $`kc = \pi`$ (the Z point):

```math
E_g = \sqrt{\Delta^2 + 4(t_s - t_l)^2} \;\approx\; \Delta + \frac{2(t_s - t_l)^2}{\Delta}
```

Since $`t_s - t_l \propto \delta`$, the gap shift goes as $`\delta^2 \propto P_s^2`$. Two takeaways:

1. The gap depends on the **square** of the displacement, so it doesn't care about the sign of $`P_s`$ (which domain direction you are in). That is inversion symmetry showing up in $`E_g`$.
2. Apply a field: $`\delta \to \delta_0 + \delta' E`$. Then $`E_g \propto (\delta_0 + \delta' E)^2`$ has a term **linear in $`E`$**, equal to $`2\delta_0\delta' E`$. It exists only when $`\delta_0 \neq 0`$, i.e. only in the ferroelectric phase. This is a cartoon of why the Pockels effect scales with $`P_s`$ and vanishes in the paraelectric phase (matching 4.0).

Same idea in tensor language. Write the quadratic EO effect as $`\Delta(1/n^2)_{ij} = g_{ijkl}P_kP_l`$ and put $`P = P_s + \Delta P`$ with $`\Delta P = \epsilon_0(\varepsilon_{33}-1)E`$:

```math
\Delta\!\left(\frac{1}{n^2}\right) \approx 2g\,P_s\,\Delta P
\;\Rightarrow\;
r_{33} \approx 2\,g_{33}\,\epsilon_0\,(\varepsilon_{33}-1)\,P_s
```

Here $`g_{33}`$ is the $`zzzz`$ component. SBN's very large low-frequency $`\varepsilon_{33}`$ (hundreds to thousands, soft-mode dominated) is the main reason $`r_{33}`$ is so big.

### 6.5 Orbital picture: crystal-field splitting in C4v

The conduction band edge is mostly Nb 4d, and the valence band is mostly O 2p with some Nb 4d hybridization. In an ideal octahedron ($`O_h`$) the d orbitals split into $`t_{2g}`$ (3-fold: $`d_{xy}, d_{xz}, d_{yz}`$) and $`e_g`$ ($`d_{z^2}, d_{x^2-y^2}`$). Lowering to $`C_{4v}`$ (distortion along $`z`$) splits them further:

| Octahedral | C4v irreps |
|---|---|
| t2g | b2 (dxy) + e (dxz, dyz) |
| eg | a1 (dz2) + b1 (dx2-y2) |

Caveat: the real Nb sites in the TTB cell have lower local symmetry than C4v, so treat this as intuition for an idealized octahedron.

### 6.6 From bands to the permittivity tensor

Start from Liu's two-level result (Eq. 1.165, **without** the rotating-wave approximation, since Liu says to avoid RWA off-resonance). With $`\gamma \to 0`$:

```math
\chi(\omega) = \frac{p_{12}p_{21}\,(N_1 - N_2)}{\epsilon_0\hbar}
\left[\frac{1}{\omega+\omega_{21}} - \frac{1}{\omega-\omega_{21}}\right]
= \frac{2\omega_{21}\,\lvert p_{12}\rvert^2\,(N_1-N_2)}{\epsilon_0\hbar\,(\omega_{21}^2 - \omega^2)}
```

For tensors, note that $`p_{12}p_{21}`$ is really a **dyad** $`(\mathbf p_{12})_i(\mathbf p_{21})_j`$. For a solid, replace the single transition by a sum over valence-conduction pairs and integrate over the BZ (the idea of Liu Eq. 1.144). Up to a spin factor of 2:

```math
\varepsilon_{ij}(\omega) = \delta_{ij} + \frac{2}{\epsilon_0\hbar}
\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}
\sum_{c,v}
\frac{\omega_{cv}(\mathbf k)\,\big[\mathbf p_{vc}(\mathbf k)\big]_i\big[\mathbf p_{cv}(\mathbf k)\big]_j\,\big(f_v - f_c\big)}
{\omega_{cv}^2(\mathbf k) - \omega^2}
```

with $`\hbar\omega_{cv}(\mathbf k) = E_c(\mathbf k) - E_v(\mathbf k)`$. All the anisotropy of $`\varepsilon_{ij}`$ sits in two places: the shape of $`E_c(\mathbf k) - E_v(\mathbf k)`$ (the lattice geometry) and the direction dependence of the matrix elements $`\mathbf p_{cv}`$ (the orbital symmetry).

**Selection rules.** The dipole operator transforms as $`z \to A_1`$ and $`(x,y) \to E`$ in $`C_{4v}`$. A transition $`\Gamma_v \to \Gamma_c`$ is allowed for $`z`$-polarization only if $`\Gamma_v\otimes\Gamma_c`$ contains $`A_1`$, and for $`x,y`$ only if it contains $`E`$. Examples: $`A_1\otimes A_1 = A_1`$ (allowed for $`\mathbf E\parallel z`$), $`A_1\otimes E = E`$ (allowed for $`\mathbf E\perp z`$), $`B_1\otimes B_2 = A_2`$ (forbidden for both).

So $`z`$-polarized and in-plane-polarized light couple to **different sets of transitions** with different $`\omega_{cv}`$ and oscillator strengths. In Sellmeier form:

```math
n_o^2(\omega) = 1 + \sum_s \frac{A^{\perp}_s}{(\omega_s^{\perp})^{2} - \omega^2},
\qquad
n_e^2(\omega) = 1 + \sum_s \frac{A^{\parallel}_s}{(\omega_s^{\parallel})^{2} - \omega^2}
```

That is the origin of $`n_o \neq n_e`$. The electronic structure of the octahedral framework is only slightly distorted from cubic-like, so the two sets are close and $`\Delta n \sim 0.01`$.

The optical index comes from the electronic response only. SBN's huge low-frequency $`\varepsilon_{33}`$ has an extra **ionic** (soft phonon) contribution that is not in this formula, and that is what boosts $`r_{33}`$ (6.4).

## 7. Summary: what 4mm allows

| Property | Rank | Surviving components | Independent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spontaneous polarization | 1 | Pz | 1 |
| Permittivity, permeability, 1/m* | 2 (symmetric) | diag(a, a, b) | 2 |
| d (SHG) | 3 | d15 = d24, d31 = d32, d33 | 3 (2 with Kleinman) |
| r (Pockels) | 3 | r13 = r23, r33, r42 = r51 | 3 |
| Elastic stiffness | 4 | c11, c12, c13, c33, c44, c66 | 6 |

Suggested order: Neumann's principle, then parity counting, then the index ellipsoid, then the band toys. Do 6.4 on paper at least once, it's the piece that links symmetry to the gap.
